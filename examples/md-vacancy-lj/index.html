Checking against Yao and Olvera de la Cruz

Their 2014 study (Phys. Rev. E 90, 062318) tracked one-, two- and three-point vacancies in a 20,000-atom LJ crystal at zero pressure and found the two-point vacancy diffuses fastest. Here the same question is asked of many tiny 10×10 crystals run side by side, each holding one vacancy cluster, with the lattice set to its zero-pressure spacing at each temperature. Dashed lines are the slopes they report; solid curves are ensembles run with this code.

Differences from the paper, so agreement is qualitative: 99 atoms per replica instead of 20,000 (they found size effects vanish above about 12,800); a fixed box at the zero-pressure spacing a(T) measured with this code, rather than a Nosé–Hoover barostat; a Berendsen thermostat; and shorter runs. The vacancy position is the centroid of 1 − occupancy over lattice sites, which stays defined when a three-point vacancy changes shape.
